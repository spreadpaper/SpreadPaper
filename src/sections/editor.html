The editor

Place the image once, on a canvas shaped like your desk

Drop a photograph on the canvas and it lands under an outline of your displays, in the arrangement macOS already knows about. Drag it around and it snaps to the middle and to the edges as it passes them.

Zoom, fit, flip

A small set of controls floats over the bottom of the canvas: zoom between 10 and 300 per cent, fit the image to the canvas in one click, and mirror it horizontally when the composition reads better the other way round.

One file for every screen

Each display gets its own rendered file, keyed on the display ID rather than the name, so two identical monitors never end up sharing one picture. Wallpapers go up one at a time, so a second click cannot overtake the first.

Look before you save

Preview puts the wallpaper on your screens with the editor still open, so you can judge it where it will live. Save keeps it as a preset, and Save & Apply does both at once.

Bezel compensation

Monitors have bezels, and on a spread wallpaper those bezels sit right in the middle of the picture. SpreadPaper pushes the display frames apart by the width of the housing between them, so the photograph carries on behind each bezel rather than being drawn on both screens at once. A ridgeline then runs straight across the gap.

That is a trade you are making on purpose: the strip behind each bezel is part of the image you never see. Set one width for all your displays or a pair for each of them, horizontal for the left and right edges, vertical for the top and bottom, measured in points. The controls appear once a second display is connected, and Settings holds the gap new displays start from.
